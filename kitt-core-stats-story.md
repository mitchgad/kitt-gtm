# Kitt core stats — the adherence story and the five we use

Distilled from the full research pass in [[kitt-stats-bank-2026-08-04]] (4 Aug 2026). Signed direction from Mitch: Sinsky for admin hours; the five stats below are the working set. Related: [[kitt-product-knowledge]], [[allied-health-audience]].

**Framing guardrail:** every stat is third-party research. Always "research shows / the study found…", never "Kitt achieves…". No clinical-outcome claims for Kitt.

---

## The adherence story (the Companion narrative, end-to-end)

When a physio prescribes home exercises, research shows non-adherence can reach 70% — clients not doing their program as prescribed between sessions (Essery 2017 systematic review, restated in a 2024 umbrella review of 205 trials).

This matters clinically because adherence drives results: a 2025 *Journal of Physiotherapy* analysis of 46 trials found clients who did 80–100% of their prescribed exercise had meaningfully less pain and disability, while 60–79% adherence showed no significant benefit. Partial compliance isn't half the result — it's often no result.

Part of why adherence collapses: patients forget 40–80% of what a clinician tells them immediately, and nearly half of what they do remember is wrong (Kessels 2003).

The fix has evidence too: an Australian RCT (Melbourne, knee OA) found an app delivering the program more than doubled complete adherence — 36% vs 16%.

That's the story: **clients don't remember, so they don't do it; when they don't do it, they don't improve; putting the plan in their pocket demonstrably lifts adherence.**

## The five stats

### 1. Admin — the Sinsky stat
Physicians spend ~2 hours on documentation and desk work for every 1 hour of direct patient care, plus 1–2 hours of after-hours work each night.
- Sinsky C, et al. *Annals of Internal Medicine* 2016;165(11):753–760. doi:10.7326/M16-0961
- Label as physician research (US, 4 specialties) — no AU physio equivalent exists, so this is the category benchmark.

### 2. Adherence — the 70%
Non-adherence to home-based physical therapy can reach 70%.
- Essery R, et al. *Disability and Rehabilitation* 2017;39(6):519–534. doi:10.3109/09638288.2016.1153160
- Corroborated: Ley C, Putz P. *Systematic Reviews* 2024;13:137. doi:10.1186/s13643-024-02538-9
- This is home-exercise non-adherence, not treatment dropout. Replaces the uncitable "70% of clients fail to complete treatment" wording on the Companion page.

### 3. Adherence → outcomes
High adherence (80–100%) is associated with significantly less pain (MD −14.32/100) and fewer functional limitations (MD −8.08/100) in chronic low back pain; moderate adherence (60–79%) showed no significant association.
- Jones MD, et al. *Journal of Physiotherapy* 2025;71(2). doi:10.1016/j.jphys.2025.03.004 (46 RCTs, secondary analysis of a Cochrane review)
- Association, not causation; low certainty — keep to "linked to / associated with" phrasing.

### 4. Forgetting — the hook stat
40–80% of medical information provided by clinicians is forgotten immediately, and almost half of what is remembered is remembered incorrectly.
- Kessels RPC. *J R Soc Med* 2003;96(5):219–222. doi:10.1258/jrsm.96.5.219
- General medical consultations, not physio-specific (figure originates with Ley 1988).

### 5. Apps work — the Australian RCT
An app delivering the exercise program more than doubled complete adherence at 26 weeks: 36% vs 16% (RR 2.1, p<0.001), in Australian adults with knee osteoarthritis.
- Hinman RS, Nelligan RK, et al. (MappKO trial). *Osteoarthritis and Cartilage* 2026. doi:10.1016/j.joca.2025.12.012 (n=182)
- Use for adherence ONLY — the trial found no difference in function (WOMAC), so never pair this stat with an outcomes claim.
