# Kitt Stats Bank — 2026-08-04

Research pass run 4 Aug 2026 (three parallel research agents + direct verification of the APA 2025 Workforce Census PDF and the UCLA ambient-scribe RCT). Every stat below is traced to a primary source. Confidence tiers:

- **VERIFIED** — figure read directly from the primary source (or its indexed abstract) this session.
- **SECONDARY** — figure consistent across secondary sources but primary not directly opened; confirm before publishing.
- **DO NOT USE** — popular stat found to be apocryphal, fabricated, or contested.

**Framing guardrail (from [[kitt-product-knowledge]]):** every stat is third-party evidence — always "research shows / the study found X", never "Kitt achieves X". No clinical-outcome claims for Kitt. Related: [[allied-health-audience]], [[health-tech-gtm-strategy]].

## 2. Admin & documentation burden (→ Kitt Clinician notes/letters)

**Headline finding: no published AU figure exists for physio/EP hours-per-week on admin.** The APA 2025 Workforce Census (read in full this session) contains no such metric; nor do ESSA, AHPA or Ahpra publications. Kitt's "9→2 hrs/week" and "7+ hrs saved" claims cannot be externally benchmarked — they must stand as Kitt's own product claims, and category evidence below is the substantiation layer.

| # | Stat | Source | Confidence |
|---|------|--------|------------|
| 2.1 | Physicians spend **~2 hours on EHR/desk work for every 1 hour of direct patient care**; only 27% of the office day is face time | Sinsky C, et al. *Annals of Internal Medicine* 2016;165(11):753–760. doi:10.7326/M16-0961. US physicians, 4 specialties — category evidence, not allied health. | VERIFIED |
| 2.2 | Physicians add **1–2 hours of after-hours clerical work per night** ("pyjama time") | Sinsky 2016 (as above; 21/57 diary subsample) | VERIFIED |
| 2.3 | 33.6% of US family-medicine residents report 3+ hrs/night after-hours EHR work; heavy EHR users show higher burnout (65.0% vs 49.5%) | Barr W, et al. *Ann Fam Med* 2024;22(Suppl 1):6628. doi:10.1370/afm.22.s1.6628. n=3,931. Conference-abstract level. | VERIFIED |
| 2.4 | Australian allied health assistants spend 14–21% of the working day on admin + clinical reporting (~2.5 hrs of a ~7.7-hr day) | Snowdon DA, et al. *BMC Health Services Research* 2022;22:1280. doi:10.1186/s12913-022-08642-7. n=51 AHAs, Victorian public health services — assistants, not clinicians. | VERIFIED |
| 2.5 | APA 2025 Workforce Census: among physios planning to exit within 5 years, **poor remuneration 60% (up from 45%) and mental burnout 35% (up from 32%)** are the top reasons; burnout hits 44% of foundation-level and 40% of women; the report explicitly names **NDIS administrative burden** as an exacerbating factor | APA 2025 Workforce Census (Survey Matters, Apr 2026), PDF verified this session: australian.physio/sites/default/files/APA-2025-Workforce-Census-Report-Apr26.pdf | VERIFIED |
| 2.6 | APA member survey: **31% of physiotherapists are considering ceasing NDIS work**; "the administrative burden associated with NDIS is significantly higher than that for private clients" (APA President) | APA media release "Third of physiotherapists consider exiting NDIS due to untenable pricing" — australian.physio/media/. Pricing is the headline driver; admin burden secondary. | SECONDARY |
| 2.7 | Kaiser Permanente ambient AI scribes: **15,791 physician-hours of documentation time saved** across 7,260 physicians / ~2.5M encounters (Oct 2023–Dec 2024); 84% said it improved patient connection | *NEJM Catalyst* 2025, doi:10.1056/CAT.25.0040 (paywalled; figures cross-confirmed via permanente.org + AMA coverage). **DO NOT USE (fabricated):**
- *"AHPA 2021 survey: allied health professionals spend 9 hrs/week on admin; 60% high stress"* — traces only to a vendor blog with no source link; no such AHPA publication exists. Its resemblance to Kitt's own "9 hours" framing makes it extra dangerous — citing it would launder an unsourced number.
- The SoftwareFinder "85% pyjama-time / 8.2 hrs week" stat — vendor-marketing site, underlying survey untraceable.

## Implications for existing site claims (proposals, not decisions)

1. **"70% of clients fail to complete treatment" (Companion page)** — not citable as written. Proposal: re-word toward "research shows non-adherence to home exercise programs can reach 70%" (Essery 2017 / Ley & Putz 2024). Needs Mitch's call.
2. **"9→2 admin hrs/week" and "81% less documentation time" (homepage)** — no external benchmark exists; these must be presented as Kitt's own measurements. If they came from user data, worth documenting the basis internally; if not, consider softening.
3. The Sinsky "2 hours of paperwork per hour of care" and Kessels "40–80% forgotten" lines are the safest high-impact third-party stats for top-of-funnel copy (with "physician" / "medical consultation" context labels kept honest).
4. The UCLA RCT (2.8) is double-edged: it validates ambient documentation for burnout relief but shows time-savings are tool-dependent — useful against scribe competitors, but invites "where's Kitt's data?" so deploy carefully.
